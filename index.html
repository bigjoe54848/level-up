How eating works on this plan

Build your plateHalf the plate veggies or fruit, a quarter protein (chicken, eggs, beans, fish, tofu), a quarter whole grains or potatoes/rice. You don't need to measure anything — just aim for roughly that balance most meals.
Actually eat breakfastSkipping it usually means you're starving and snacking on whatever's fastest by 3pm. Toast with butter, a banana, and eggs — what you're already eating — keeps your energy steady and makes it way easier to make good choices later in the day.
Water over sodaSwap sugary drinks for water most of the time. It's one of the easiest changes that actually moves the needle, and it's free.
Nothing is bannedPizza night, dessert, fries with friends — all fine. This plan is about what you eat most of the time, not perfect eating. Restricting foods completely usually backfires.
Sleep is part of the planAim for 8–10 hours. Your body builds strength and recovers while you sleep, especially during the growth spurts of your teens — it matters as much as the workouts do.

Working with a freezer, not a fridge

Nuggets, pizza, and patties are fineThey're not "bad" foods. The easiest upgrade isn't replacing them — it's adding a frozen veggie side (mixed veg, corn, peas — usually under $2 a bag and lasts forever) or a piece of fruit next to them.
How to just cook a steakPat it dry, salt and pepper both sides. Pan on medium-high with a little oil, about 3–4 minutes a side for a normal-thickness steak (thinner = less time). It's done medium when it feels like the base of your thumb when you touch your thumb to your middle finger — firmer than that and it's overcooking. Let it sit 5 minutes before cutting. Cook extra so you've got leftovers for lunch.
Mozzarella sticks aren't a full mealThey're better as a snack or a side than as dinner by themselves — pair a few with something else, like nuggets or a sandwich.
A short list to ask your parents forBread, butter, bananas, and eggs — sounds like you've usually got these, so just keeping them stocked covers breakfast. Everything else — dinners especially — you can already build from rice, frozen veggies, and what's in the freezer.

How the training works

Rest between setsTake 45–60 seconds to catch your breath between each exercise in a round. This isn't a race.
Form over repsA smaller, controlled movement beats a sloppy full-range one. If your form breaks down, that's your real rep count for the day — stop there.
Some soreness is normalFeeling it in your muscles a day or two after a new workout is normal. Sharp pain in a joint is not — back off and rest if that happens, and tell an adult if it doesn't go away.
Missed a day? No big dealJust pick the plan back up on the next day — don't try to double up to "catch up." Consistency over weeks beats any single workout.
This is a general training and habit guide, not medical or nutrition advice tailored to you individually. Talk to a parent/guardian and a doctor before starting, particularly about what's an appropriate pace of change for you while you're still growing.
